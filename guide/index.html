1. What is SIP?

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) is a method of investing a fixed amount in a mutual fund every month — automatically, on a date you choose. It's the most recommended way to invest in equity mutual funds in India.

Why SIP beats one-time investment for most people

  • Rupee Cost Averaging: You buy more units when markets are low and fewer when high — automatically reducing your average cost over time.
  • Discipline: Money leaves your account before you can spend it.
  • No timing needed: You don't need to predict when markets will rise or fall.
  • Starts at ₹100/month: No large upfront capital required.
Example: ₹5,000/month SIP for 20 years at 12% returns = ₹49.9 lakh. Total invested: only ₹12 lakh. That's 4× your money — purely from compounding.

2. How SIP Returns Are Calculated (Formula)

The SIP maturity value is calculated using the future value of annuity formula:

SIP Future Value Formula
FV = P × ((1 + r)^n − 1) / r × (1 + r)

Where:
P = Monthly SIP amount (₹)
r = Monthly return rate = Annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
n = Number of months

Worked Example: ₹5,000/month at 12% for 10 Years

Step 1 — Monthly rate: r = 12 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.01

Step 2 — Months: n = 10 × 12 = 120

Step 3 — Apply formula:

FV = 5,000 × ((1.01)^120 − 1) / 0.01 × (1.01)
= 5,000 × (3.3004 − 1) / 0.01 × 1.01
= 5,000 × 230.04 × 1.01
= ₹11,61,695

📊 ₹5,000/month SIP at 12% — 10 Year Result

Total Invested₹6,00,000
Returns (Gains)₹5,61,695
Maturity Value₹11,61,695
Wealth Multiple1.94× your money

Notice that the formula includes a final (1 + r) multiplier — this accounts for the fact that the last SIP instalment also earns one month of return before maturity. This is why SIP calculators show slightly different numbers from naive calculations.

The power of time: At the same 12% return, extending the same ₹5,000/month SIP from 10 to 20 years grows maturity from ₹11.6L to ₹49.9L — 4.3× more money for only 2× more investment period. This is compounding at work.

4. Step-Up SIP — The Wealth Multiplier

Step-Up SIP (also called Top-Up SIP) means increasing your monthly SIP amount by a fixed percentage every year. Most people's salary grows 8–12% annually — Step-Up SIP ensures your investment grows with your income.

🚀 Regular SIP vs Step-Up SIP — 20 year comparison

Regular SIP (₹5,000 flat)₹49.9 lakh
Step-Up SIP (₹5,000 + 10%/yr)₹1.01 crore
Extra wealth from Step-Up+₹51 lakh (2×)

How to set up Step-Up SIP

  1. Most AMCs (Groww, Zerodha Coin, etc.) let you enable Top-Up SIP while creating a new SIP.
  2. Set the step-up % to your expected annual salary increment (typically 8–15%).
  3. Or increase manually every April — set a calendar reminder.

6. LTCG Tax on Mutual Funds (Budget 2024)

After Budget 2024, equity mutual fund gains are taxed as follows:

Gain TypeHolding PeriodTax RateExemption
LTCG (Long Term)More than 1 year12.5%₹1.25 lakh/year
STCG (Short Term)1 year or less20%None
Debt MFAny periodAs per slabNone

Smart tax harvesting strategy

Redeem ₹1.25 lakh of gains every financial year (before March 31) and immediately reinvest. This "harvests" the free exemption without leaving the market — your units reset to the new cost basis, reducing future tax liability.

Note: For SIPs, each monthly instalment has its own purchase date. Units bought less than 1 year ago attract STCG (20%). Check with a CA for large redemptions.

7. How to Pick the Right Mutual Fund

For beginners

  • Nifty 50 Index Fund — Lowest cost (0.1–0.2% expense ratio), mirrors the market. Ideal for first-time investors.
  • Flexi Cap Fund — Fund manager picks across large/mid/small cap. Good for 5+ year horizon.

For intermediate investors

  • Add Mid Cap for growth (max 20–30% of portfolio)
  • Add International Fund for currency diversification

What to check before investing

  • Expense Ratio: lower is better — Direct Plans have 0.5–1% lower expense than Regular Plans
  • Consistency: Look at 5-year and 10-year CAGR, not just 1-year returns
  • AUM: At least ₹500 crore (too small = liquidity risk)
  • Fund house: Stick to established AMCs (HDFC, ICICI, SBI, Axis, Mirae, Parag Parikh)
Always invest in Direct Plans — the same fund via your AMC website or Zerodha Coin/Groww Direct costs 0.5–1% less per year. Over 20 years, that difference is lakhs of rupees.

8. Common SIP Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Stopping SIP during market crashes — This is exactly when you should increase SIP. Crashes mean your monthly amount buys more units (rupee cost averaging).
  2. Investing in too many funds — 2–3 funds cover all diversification needs. 10 funds = overlapping portfolios + confusion.
  3. Using Regular Plans instead of Direct — Distributors earn 1%+ commission yearly from your money. Use Direct Plans on Zerodha Coin, Groww Direct, or AMC websites.
  4. Withdrawing early — SIP benefits compound most in the final years. Withdrawing at year 8 of a 15-year plan loses most of the gains.
  5. Ignoring inflation — Plan for real returns (returns minus 6% inflation). ₹1 crore in 20 years ≈ ₹30 lakh in today's purchasing power.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SIP?

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) is a method of investing a fixed amount in a mutual fund every month automatically on a date you choose. It benefits from rupee cost averaging — you buy more units when markets fall and fewer when markets rise — which lowers your average purchase cost over time.

Is SIP safe?

SIP in equity mutual funds carries market risk — returns are not guaranteed and can be negative in the short term. However, historically, equity SIPs held for 10+ years in India have delivered positive returns in almost every 10-year rolling period. The NIFTY 50 has delivered approximately 12% CAGR over the last 20 years. Debt fund SIPs are lower risk but offer lower returns (5–7%).

Can I stop my SIP in between?

Yes. You can pause or stop a SIP anytime without penalty. Units already purchased remain invested and continue to grow. However, stopping during a market crash is usually a mistake — that is precisely when rupee cost averaging works best, buying more units at lower prices.

What is CAGR and how is it different from XIRR?

CAGR (Compounded Annual Growth Rate) measures the return of a lump-sum investment. XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return) measures return for investments with multiple cash flows at different dates — which is exactly how SIPs work. For SIPs, XIRR is the correct metric. If a fund says 15% CAGR on SIP returns, it actually means 15% XIRR. Use our SIP calculator's return field to find what XIRR assumption matches your goal.

What is Step-Up SIP?

Step-Up SIP (also called Top-Up SIP) automatically increases your monthly SIP amount by a fixed percentage every year. Starting ₹5,000/month with 10% annual step-up means you invest ₹5,500 in year 2, ₹6,050 in year 3, and so on. Over 20 years, this more than doubles your maturity value compared to a flat ₹5,000/month SIP (₹1.01 crore vs ₹49.9 lakh).
